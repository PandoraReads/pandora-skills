# Grammar Patterns and Common Errors

## Tense Traps

### Present Perfect vs Simple Past
#### Common Confusions
- **Present Perfect**: Connects past to present, unfinished time
  - "I have lived here for 10 years"
  - "She has just finished her homework"

- **Simple Past**: Completed actions at specific past time
  - "I lived there in 2010"
  - "She finished her homework at 8 PM"

#### Teaching Points
- Use time markers to distinguish (since/for vs yesterday/last week)
- Emphasize unfinished vs completed time
- Provide practice with timeline visualization

### Continuous vs Simple Tenses
#### Patterns
- **Continuous**: Emphasizes action in progress
  - "I was studying when the phone rang"

- **Simple**: Emphasizes completion or habitual action
  - "I studied for three hours"
  - "I study every day"

#### Common Errors
- "I am understanding" (should be "I understand" for stative verbs)
- "He is having a car" (should be "He has a car" for possession)

### Conditional Patterns
#### Zero Conditional
- If + present simple, present simple
- Used for general truths
  - "If you heat ice, it melts"

#### First Conditional
- If + present simple, will + base verb
- Used for real possibilities
  - "If it rains, I will stay home"

#### Second Conditional
- If + past simple, would + base verb
- Used for hypothetical situations
  - "If I won the lottery, I would travel the world"

## Preposition Pitfalls

### Time Prepositions
- **At**: Specific points (at 5 PM, at noon)
- **On**: Days and dates (on Monday, on January 1st)
- **In**: Longer periods (in 2020, in summer, in the morning)

### Place Prepositions
- **In**: Inside enclosed spaces (in the house)
- **On**: Surfaces (on the table)
- **At**: Specific locations (at the corner)

### Movement Prepositions
- **To**: Direction toward (go to school)
- **From**: Direction away (come from work)
- **Into**: Entry (walk into the room)

### Common Error Pairs
- **"in time" vs "on time"**
  - "I arrived on time" (punctual)
  - "I arrived in time" (before deadline)

- **"at the end" vs "in the end"**
  - "At the end of the book"
  - "In the end, we decided to go"

## Article Usage

### Zero Article
- Plural count nouns: "dogs" (general)
- Uncountable nouns: "water" (general)
- Proper nouns: "John", "Paris"

### Definite Article
- Specific referents: "the dog I told you about"
- Unique entities: "the sun", "the president"
- Previously mentioned: "I saw a dog. The dog was brown"

### Indefinite Article
- First mention: "I saw a dog"
- Non-specific: "I need a pen"
- Professions: "She is a teacher"

#### Common Errors
- "I have cat" (should be "I have a cat")
- "The water is important" (should be "Water is important" for general statement)

## Word Order Patterns

### Question Formation
#### Statement to Question
- "You are happy" → "Are you happy?"
- "She likes coffee" → "Does she like coffee?"

#### Question Word Order
- Question word + auxiliary + subject: "What are you doing?"
- Not: "What you are doing?"

### Adjective Order
#### Correct Order
- Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose
  - "That wonderful old round blue French wooden dining table"

#### Common Errors
- "I have a blue big car" (should be "big blue car")
- "She bought a plastic new bag" (should be "new plastic bag")

## Modals and Semi-Modals

### Ability
- **Can**: Present ability ("I can swim")
- **Could**: Past ability or polite request ("I could swim when I was young")

### Possibility
- **May**: Formal possibility ("It may rain")
- **Might**: Slightly less certain ("It might rain")
- **Could**: Past possibility or polite suggestion

### Permission
- **Can**: Informal permission ("You can borrow my pen")
- **May**: Formal permission ("You may leave now")

#### Common Errors
- "I can to swim" (modal + base verb, no "to")
- "He can swimming" (modal + base verb, not -ing form)

## Phrasal Verbs

### Separable vs Inseparable
#### Separable (object can come between or after)
- "Turn on the light" or "Turn the light on"
- "Pick up the children" or "Pick the children up"

#### Inseparable (object must come after)
- "Look after the children" (not "Look the children after")
- "Run into a friend" (not "Run a friend into")

### Common Phrasal Verbs
- **Get along**: Have a good relationship
- **Give up**: Stop trying
- **Look forward to**: Anticipate
- **Put up with**: Tolerate

#### Teaching Strategy
- Teach in context, not as vocabulary lists
- Show separability patterns
- Use visual scenes to illustrate meaning

## Error Detection and Correction

### Error Categories
1. **Developmental Errors**: Natural part of learning process
2. **Interference Errors**: From first language
3. **Overgeneralization**: Applying rules too broadly
4. **Fossilization**: Persistent errors despite instruction

### Correction Strategies
- Focus on meaning first, form second
- Provide input, not just correction
- Create opportunities for self-correction
- Address systemic patterns, not individual errors

### Contextual Error Analysis
- Identify when errors occur (free writing vs. structured task)
- Note error patterns (consistent vs. random)
- Consider cognitive load at time of error
- Link to specific grammar structures being learned
